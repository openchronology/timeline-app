# OpenChronology — Tour: Deep Time. Script and Storyboard

**Runtime:** ~1:49 (the scratch narration's cut) · **Format:** the real app, captured, in a browser frame, with infographic callouts; **Strata** look · **Aspect:** 16:9
**Audience:** science teachers, museum and outreach educators, geology and biology students, and anyone who has tried to picture 4.5 billion years. They know Ma and Ga; they're used to diagrams that cheat with broken axes.
**Purpose:** take one unbroken zoom from Earth's formation down to a single second in 1969, stopping at the eons, the Phanerozoic, the human lineage and recorded history, and show that it's all one exact line, with no broken axis and no rounding.

**How it differs:** almost all of it is one camera move. The narration names what appears; the numbers on the ruler do the convincing. It ends by changing the display to dates, so the same exact moment reads as "1969-07-20 20:17:40".

**Production notes:**

- Narration is 245 words, about 140 wpm when read; unhurried, with room for the zoom to land.
- **The timeline is the sample `publicity/video/samples/deep-time.ochx`** (`deep-time.mjs`): eons and eras as durations in the International Chronostratigraphic Chart's colours; moments for formation, the oldest minerals, early life, oxygen, Snowball Earth, the Cambrian, plants on land, Tiktaalik, the Great Dying, the first dinosaurs, the asteroid, hominins, Homo sapiens, farming, writing, the Great Pyramid and Apollo 11. Times are exact offsets from 2000 CE, shown in millions of years.
- **Accuracy guardrails:**
  - Ages are rounded figures from standard references (Appendix B); the eon and era boundaries are the ICS chart's (v2023/09). Say "about" for everything before writing.
  - "Exactly 66 million years before 2000" is a statement about the stored coordinate, not the science: the asteroid's age is known to tens of thousands of years. Don't imply otherwise.
  - The ruler reads "million years" relative to 2000 CE (negative is before); say "millions of years ago" in narration.

## ACT 1 · FOUR AND A HALF BILLION YEARS (0:00 – 0:46)

### Scene 1 · 0:00 – 0:22 · All of it

**FRAME:** The browser frame on the night-sky ground, with the whole sample after **Fit all**: the Hadean, Archean, Proterozoic and Phanerozoic as coloured bands, Earth forming at the left, early life, the Great Oxidation, and a crowded knot at the right edge. A thin bracket over that knot: "everything with bones, leaves or names".

**MOTION:** The frame rises; the eon bands' names light in order; the bracket draws last.

**OST:** "4.5 billion years. One line."

**VO:**
"This is the whole history of the Earth on one line: four and a half billion years, from its formation on the left to today on the right. The bands are the four eons. Almost everything you'd find in a museum is crammed into that last sliver at the right."

### Scene 2 · 0:22 – 0:46 · The first four billion

**FRAME:** The ruler labels and the moments of the first eons, named in turn: Earth forms (about 4,540 million years ago), the oldest known minerals, early life in stromatolites, the Great Oxidation. A callout over the Proterozoic: "Life is microbial for about 3 billion years".

**MOTION:** A slow pan across the left two thirds; each moment pulses as it's named.

**OST:** "Three billion years of microbes."

**VO:**
"The Earth forms about four and a half billion years ago. About a billion years later there's life: microbial mats that left stromatolites behind. Around two point four billion years ago, their oxygen starts to fill the air. For about three billion years, that's what life is."

---

## ACT 2 · ZOOMING IN (0:46 – 2:00)

### Scene 3 · 0:46 – 1:16 · The Phanerozoic

**FRAME:** The capture zooms into the last 540 million years: the Paleozoic, Mesozoic and Cenozoic bands; the Cambrian explosion, plants on land, Tiktaalik, the Great Dying, the first dinosaurs, the asteroid. A depth counter in the corner: "span: 4.6 billion → 600 million years".

**MOTION:** A continuous zoom; moments split out of their summaries and spread apart as the span shrinks.

**OST:** "Zoom into the last half-billion."

**VO:**
"Zoom into that sliver and it opens up. The Cambrian explosion, five hundred and thirty-nine million years ago. Plants on land. A fish walking out of the water. The Great Dying, the worst extinction we know of. The first dinosaurs. And sixty-six million years ago, the asteroid."

### Scene 4 · 1:16 – 1:38 · The human lineage

**FRAME:** A further zoom to the last seven million years: the earliest hominins, and near the right edge, Homo sapiens at about 300,000 years. The counter: "span: 8 million years".

**MOTION:** The zoom continues smoothly from the previous scene's end, as one move.

**OST:** "Seven million years of us."

**VO:**
"Keep going. The last seven million years: the earliest members of the human lineage. Our own species appears only about three hundred thousand years ago, right at the edge."

### Scene 5 · 1:38 – 2:00 · Recorded history

**FRAME:** A zoom to the last 13,000 years: farming begins, writing, the Great Pyramid. The counter: "span: 13,000 years". A small inset of Scene 1's whole line with a marker showing where we are: an invisible point at its right end.

**MOTION:** Zoom; the inset's marker pulses.

**OST:** "All of recorded history."

**VO:**
"And the last twelve thousand years: farming, then writing, then the pyramids. All of recorded history fits in a space you couldn't see at all when we started."

---

## ACT 3 · DOWN TO THE SECOND (2:00 – 2:30)

### Scene 6 · 2:00 – 2:30 · One second, exactly

**FRAME:** The zoom continues to a few seconds around Apollo 11's landing. Then **Time display** changes to Gregorian: the ruler that read in millions of years now reads 1969-07-20, 20:17, with the landing on its 40 s tick (and a panel beside the frame reads "1969-07-20 20:17:40 UTC"), and the moment hasn't moved. End card: "From eons to seconds", the ◷ wordmark. The end card also gives the source: "github.com/openchronology/timeline-app".

**MOTION:** The zoom carries on, still in millions of years, down to a few seconds around the landing: the ruler counts the last of them as offsets (Δ 7 s, Δ 8 s, Δ 9 s). On arriving, **Time display** changes to Gregorian: the same ruler now reads 1969-07-20, 20:17, and the landing sits on its 40 s tick, where it was. The end card rises and holds.

**OST:** "From eons to seconds."

**VO:**
"And still it goes: down to the second the Eagle landed on the Moon, on the same line where the Earth was formed. Change how the line is read, and nothing moves: every time is stored exactly. From eons to seconds, it's one timeline."

**[END · ~1:49]**

## Appendix C · Questions this audience is likely to ask

1. **Can I show "Ma" and "Ga" instead?** Set the unit in **Time display** (for example "Ma" with a scale of a million Julian years); a custom printer can switch units with the zoom.
2. **Why relative to 2000?** The **Geological ages** plugin records ages before 2000 CE; set the origin to 1950 for "before present" (BP) if your sources use it.
3. **How precise are these ages?** As precise as the sources (Appendix B); the timeline stores exactly what you enter and never rounds it.
4. **Can students add to it?** Yes: share it, and let them fork it or propose changes (Video 9).
