# OpenChronology — Working Together: Script and Storyboard

**Runtime:** ~2:07 (the scratch narration's cut) · **Format:** animated infographic, **Paper** look · **Aspect:** 16:9
**Audience:** teachers running a class project, and communities building one timeline together: a club's history, a fan wiki's chronology, a research group's events. They worry about who can change what, vandalism, and losing work.
**Purpose:** show the ways people share and build a timeline: links and embeds, collaborators with roles, pull requests reviewed before they land, forks that remember their source, and saves that merge rather than overwrite. End with the safety net: history and reporting.

**How it differs:** it follows one timeline ("Our town's history") through a class project, from the teacher's private draft to a public timeline improved by students' proposals, so each feature arrives when the story needs it.

**Production notes:**

- Narration is 290 words, about 140 wpm when read. Practical, reassuring.
- **The motif:** the timeline as a small card in the centre ("Our town's history"), with people as faceless figures around it, each wearing a role badge (Owner, Writer, Contributor, Viewer, Reader). Arrows for proposals, a branch line for forks.
- **Accuracy guardrails:**
  - Roles exactly as the app names them: **Viewer** reads a private timeline; **Contributor** proposes changes as pull requests; **Writer** also saves and merges or rejects; only the **owner** changes visibility, collaborators or deletes. On a public timeline anyone signed in can propose changes.
  - Accounts are for people 13 and older. Younger students can read, and **Fork in browser** to edit and export without an account.
  - Saves merge; only conflicting edits to the same thing stop and ask.

## ACT 1 · SHARING (0:00 – 0:40)

### Scene 1 · 0:00 – 0:18 · Private until you share

**FRAME:** A teacher figure (badge: OWNER) beside a timeline card "Our town's history" with a padlock. A "Sharing & settings" panel slides out with **Visibility**: "Private" switching to "Public".

**MOTION:** The padlock opens as the toggle switches; light rays from the card reach a row of small reader figures.

**OST:** "Private until you share it."

**VO:**
"Every timeline you save to a server starts private: only you can open it. When it's ready, its owner can make it public, and then anyone can read it, find it in search, and follow its history."

### Scene 2 · 0:18 – 0:40 · Links and embeds

**FRAME:** The share dialog drawn simply: **Copy link**, social buttons, **Embed on a website** with a size choice and a code box. Next to it, a class web page with the timeline embedded in it.

**MOTION:** The link copies with a tick; the embed code flies into the class page, where the timeline appears.

**OST:** "Share a link. Embed it."

**VO:**
"Share it with a link, to the whole timeline, to one moment, or to exactly the view you're looking at. Or embed it on your class page or your community's site, where readers can explore it without leaving the page."

## ACT 2 · BUILDING TOGETHER (0:40 – 1:52)

### Scene 3 · 0:40 – 1:04 · Collaborators and roles

**FRAME:** Three figures join the teacher with badges: WRITER ("saves and reviews"), CONTRIBUTOR ("proposes changes"), VIEWER ("reads"). A small table appears under them, the role names in bold, matching the app's **Collaborators** list.

**MOTION:** Each figure walks in on its role's name; its line of the table ticks in.

**OST:** "Viewer. Contributor. Writer."

**VO:**
"Add collaborators by their username, each with a role. A viewer can read a private timeline. A contributor can propose changes. A writer can save changes and review what others propose. Only the owner decides who's in, who can see it, and whether it's deleted."

### Scene 4 · 1:04 – 1:32 · Pull requests

**FRAME:** A student figure (CONTRIBUTOR) edits a copy of the card, adding two moments and a link; the save button reads **Submit pull request**. The proposal appears in a **Pull requests** list: "+2 moments, 1 relationship", a **Discussion** with one comment. The writer chooses **Merge**, and the two moments drop into the real timeline.

**MOTION:** The proposal card slides from the student to the list; a comment bubble pops; **Merge** pulses; the moments fall into place.

**OST:** "Propose. Discuss. Merge."

**VO:**
"Students don't need to be trusted with the whole timeline. As contributors, their changes become pull requests: a proposal showing what they add, remove and change, with a discussion underneath. A writer talks it through, then merges it or rejects it. On a public timeline, anyone signed in can propose."

### Scene 5 · 1:32 – 1:52 · Forks

**FRAME:** A branch line leaves the card to a second card, "Our town's history (Ana's fork)", owned by a student. Arrows between them labelled **Sync upstream** and **Propose saved changes**. A smaller grey card beside it: "Duplicate: no link back".

**MOTION:** The branch draws; an arrow pulses each way as it's named.

**OST:** "Fork it. Offer it back."

**VO:**
"Want to take it somewhere new? Fork it. A fork is your own copy that remembers where it came from: bring in the original's later changes, and offer yours back when they're ready. Students without an account can fork it in the browser and export their work."

## ACT 3 · THE SAFETY NET (1:52 – 2:30)

### Scene 6 · 1:52 – 2:14 · Saves that merge, and history

**FRAME:** Two writers save at the same moment; their edits braid into one save. Only one pair, the same moment edited two ways, shows a small dialog: **Keep my versions** · **Use the saved versions**. Beneath, a **History** list of saves, newest first.

**MOTION:** The two edit streams merge; the conflict dialog pops for the one pair; the history list stacks up.

**OST:** "Nothing is overwritten."

**VO:**
"When two people save at once, their edits merge. Only when both changed the same thing differently does it stop and ask whose to keep. And every save is a revision in the timeline's history, so you can always go back to how it was."

### Scene 7 · 2:14 – 2:30 · Build it together

**FRAME:** The whole cast around the card, now public, with a **Report** flag icon small at the edge ("Report: to the site's moderators"). End card: "Build it together", the ◷ wordmark. The end card also gives the source: "github.com/openchronology/timeline-app".

**MOTION:** The card glows; the end card rises and holds.

**OST:** "Build it together."

**VO:**
"And if something doesn't belong, anyone can report it to the site's moderators. Start a timeline, invite your class or your community, and build it together."

## Appendix C · Questions this audience is likely to ask

1. **My students are under 13.** They can read public timelines and use **Fork in browser** (no account) to edit and export; only people 13 and older can sign up.
2. **Can a reviewer ask for changes?** In the proposal's **Discussion**; its author updates it (**Rebase on upstream**, or a new save from their fork), and a writer then merges or rejects it.
3. **Can I see who changed what?** Every save is a revision in **History**; proposals keep their author and discussion.
4. **Can I limit an embed to certain sites?** The server's operator can (`EMBED_FRAME_ANCESTORS`).
