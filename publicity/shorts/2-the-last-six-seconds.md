# OpenChronology — Short 2: You're the Last Six Seconds. Script and Storyboard

**Runtime:** ~0:36 · **Shape:** the stat · **Look:** Strata · **Aspect:** 9:16
**For:** science and space feeds: enthusiasts, students, the curious. They've heard "4.5 billion years" so often it means nothing; a day on a clock they can feel.
**Purpose:** squeeze Earth's history into one day, land the number they'll repeat (our species is the last six seconds), then show the real thing: the app holding the same history at true scale, eons to seconds on one line.
**Points to:** Video 8, deep time.

**Production notes:**

- About 95 words, quick but with room after the six seconds.
- The clock is drawn, not captured: a 24-hour dial whose hand sweeps through the day, each event a tick on the rim with its time.
- **The app shot is the sample `publicity/video/samples/deep-time.ochx`**, captured at phone size and shown in `phoneShot`.
- All the times are worked out in Appendix B; say "around", "near" and "about", as written.

## ACT 1 · ONE DAY (0:00 – 0:25)

### Scene 1 · 0:00 – 0:05 · Midnight

**FRAME:** A large 24-hour dial filling the middle of the frame, its hand at midnight, "00:00" under it, and small along the rim the hours. A label at the top of the dial: "Earth forms". Already whole at frame 0.

**MOTION:** None to start; the hand begins to sweep on "single day".

**OST:** "If Earth's history were one day…"

**VO:**
"Squeeze all four and a half billion years of Earth's history into a single day."

### Scene 2 · 0:05 – 0:17 · The day goes by

**FRAME:** The hand sweeps; as it passes each, a tick on the rim lights with its label and clock time: "Life · 5:36", "Oxygen · 11:19", "Animals · 21:09", "Asteroid · 23:39". The digital time under the dial runs with the hand.

**MOTION:** The sweep slows a little at each tick, which pops in on its word.

**OST:** "If Earth's history were one day…"

**VO:**
"Life shows up around half past five in the morning. Oxygen fills the air near noon. Animals take off after nine at night. The asteroid hits the dinosaurs about twenty minutes before midnight."

### Scene 3 · 0:17 – 0:25 · The last six seconds

**FRAME:** The dial's last minute magnified into a second dial: a seconds track from 23:59:00 to midnight. A bright sliver at the very end: "Homo sapiens · last 6 s". Then a hairline at the edge of it: "Writing · last 0.1 s".

**MOTION:** The magnifier grows out of the dial's top; the sliver fills on "six"; the hairline draws on "tenth".

**OST:** "We're the last six seconds"

**VO:**
"And our whole species? The last six seconds. Everything ever written down: the last tenth of a second."

## Appendix B · Claims and where they come from

A day of 86,400 s stands for 4.54 billion years; an event _a_ years ago falls at 86,400 × (1 − _a_ / 4.54 × 10⁹) s after midnight.

| Scene | Claim                                 | Event and age                                         | Clock time       | Source                                                                       |
| ----- | ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Four and a half billion years         | Earth, ~4.54 Ga                                       | 00:00            | [Age of Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Earth)                   |
| 2     | Life "around half past five"          | Stromatolites, ~3.48 Ga                               | 05:36            | [Stromatolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stromatolite)                   |
| 2     | Oxygen "near noon"                    | Great Oxidation Event, ~2.4 Ga                        | 11:19            | [Great Oxidation Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Oxidation_Event) |
| 2     | Animals "after nine at night"         | Cambrian explosion, 538.8 Ma                          | 21:09            | [ICS chart](https://stratigraphy.org/chart)                                  |
| 2     | Asteroid "about twenty minutes" to go | K–Pg, 66 Ma                                           | 23:39 (20.9 min) | ICS chart                                                                    |
| 3     | Our species "the last six seconds"    | Homo sapiens, ~300 ka                                 | 5.7 s to go      | [Jebel Irhoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebel_Irhoud)                   |
