# OpenChronology — Tour: The United States, 1775–2008. Script and Storyboard

**Runtime:** ~1:52 (the scratch narration's cut) · **Format:** the real app, captured, in a browser frame, with infographic callouts; **Archive** look · **Aspect:** 16:9
**Audience:** students and history teachers; anyone who learns history as a sequence of causes. Teachers will judge it as classroom material, so it must be accurate and calm.
**Purpose:** show a modern-history timeline the way a class would use it: the whole sweep, a war up close, one thread (rights) pulled out of the rest, a moment read with its sources, and a zoom from two and a half centuries down to the hours of Apollo 11. Then point teachers at what they can do with it.

**How it differs:** it's a tour, not a lesson in controls. The subject leads and the app follows; every control is named once, in passing, by what it does for the history.

**Production notes:**

- Narration is 255 words, about 140 wpm when read, in the voice of a good history teacher: curious, not breathless.
- **The timeline is the sample `publicity/video/samples/usa.ochx`**, built from `usa.mjs`: 48 moments, 11 durations, 16 relationships, Wikipedia sources on each, the Timeline theme in archive colours. Shots are captured from the real editor in full screen (`shots.json` in the video's folder) and shown in a browser frame; the infographic layer adds titles and arrows around the frame, never over the app's own text.
- **Accuracy guardrails:**
  - Only well-established dates (Appendix B). Apollo 11's times are NASA's mission times in UTC.
  - Say "a sample timeline we built" — it's not presented as a definitive history.
  - Account and classroom claims: free, no account to read a public timeline; accounts are for people 13 and older, so younger students read and export but don't sign up.

## ACT 1 · TWO AND A HALF CENTURIES (0:00 – 0:58)

### Scene 1 · 0:00 – 0:22 · The whole sweep

**FRAME:** The browser frame fills most of the screen, showing the sample after **Fit all**: 1775 to 2008, summaries, the Civil War and Cold War bands; the chapter headings show as an inset (at this distance they fold into summaries, and appear as you zoom). To its left, a vertical strip of three chips appears: "48 moments", "11 durations", "16 links".

**MOTION:** The frame rises into place; the chips tick in on the narration's numbers.

**OST:** "1775 to 2008, on one line."

**VO:**
"Here's a sample timeline we built of the United States, from the first shots of the Revolution to 2008. Wars and movements are bands, everything else is a moment, and each one links to the article it draws on. Chapter headings mark the eras."

### Scene 2 · 0:22 – 0:58 · A war, up close

**FRAME:** The capture zooms from the whole sweep into 1860–1866: the Civil War band fills the width, with Fort Sumter, the Emancipation Proclamation, Gettysburg (a three-day band), the Gettysburg Address, Appomattox and Lincoln's assassination drawn apart. A callout at the edge: "Summaries open as you zoom".

**MOTION:** A continuous zoom; the summary dots split into titled moments as they spread.

**OST:** "Zoom into a war."

**VO:**
"Zoom into the 1860s and the Civil War opens up. Fort Sumter, the Emancipation Proclamation, the three days of Gettysburg, Lincoln's address there that November, the surrender at Appomattox, and five days later, his assassination. At this distance you can see how close together they were."

## ACT 2 · THREADS AND SOURCES (0:58 – 1:46)

### Scene 3 · 0:58 – 1:22 · One thread: rights

**FRAME:** Back to the whole sweep. **Separate by tags** with "rights" chosen: a second track rises above the first, holding the Declaration, the Bill of Rights, the Thirteenth to Fifteenth Amendments, Seneca Falls, the Nineteenth, Brown v. Board, the Civil Rights and Voting Rights Acts. Arcs link each step to the next.

**MOTION:** The tagged moments lift into their own track; the arcs between them draw in left to right.

**OST:** "Pull out one thread."

**VO:**
"Pull one thread out of the rest. Separate by tags, choose rights, and every moment about who the founding promises applied to moves into a track of its own: from the Declaration, through the amendments after the Civil War, to Brown versus Board and the Civil Rights Act."

### Scene 4 · 1:22 – 1:46 · Read a moment, follow its links

**FRAME:** With **View mode** on, Brown v. Board of Education opens in the reader, over the app: the date, the tag, the notes, a Sources list with its link, and "Related to": Rosa Parks arrested, the Civil Rights Act. Choosing the Civil Rights Act swaps the reader to it.

**MOTION:** The reader opens over the app; a pointer chooses the related entry; the reader's content cross-fades.

**OST:** "Every moment has its sources."

**VO:**
"Open a moment to read it. Brown versus Board of Education: what the Court decided, where that comes from, and what it's linked to. Follow a link to the Civil Rights Act ten years later, and keep going."

"And the same line goes all the way down. Zoom from two and a half centuries to a single night in July 1969: the Eagle lands at seventeen minutes and forty seconds past eight, UTC, and six and a half hours later, the first step."

## Appendix C · Questions this audience is likely to ask

1. **Can my students use it without accounts?** Yes, to read public timelines and to make one in the browser and export it. Students 13 and older can sign up to save to the server.
2. **Can I use this sample?** Yes: import `usa.ochx`; then edit it, or publish it from your own account.
3. **Can I add pictures?** Yes, with the **Moment icons** and **Duration backgrounds** plugins (the **History education** suite installs them together); link images you may share.
4. **What stops students vandalizing a shared timeline?** Only writers can save; contributors propose changes for review. See Video 9.
