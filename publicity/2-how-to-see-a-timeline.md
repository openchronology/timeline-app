# OpenChronology — How to See a Timeline: Script and Storyboard

**Runtime:** ~1:58 (the scratch narration's cut) · **Format:** animated infographic, **Paper** look · **Aspect:** 16:9, title-safe for 1:1 and 9:16
**Audience:** readers: students, people following a shared link, anyone who will look at timelines more than make them. Assume a phone as often as a laptop, and no account.
**Purpose:** in two and a half minutes, teach someone to read any timeline: what each mark means, how to move around, and how to open what's there. They should be able to explore a public timeline straight afterwards.

**How it differs:** it's a lesson, not a pitch. Every gesture is shown twice, mouse and touch, and every mark is named with the app's own word (moment, duration, summary, relationship). No editing is shown; that's for the tours.

**Production notes:**

- Narration is 278 words, about 140 wpm when read, slightly slower than the others: it's teaching.
- **Gestures:** draw a mouse cursor and a fingertip side by side in a small inset at the lower right whenever a gesture is described; the main frame shows its effect.
- **The motif:** one sample timeline ("A voyage", eight moments, two durations, one relationship) stays on screen and is explored for the whole video, so the viewer watches one timeline become readable.
- **Accuracy guardrails:**
  - A summary groups moments too close together on screen; **Group within** (12–48 px) sets how close. Moments at exactly the same time never separate.
  - The reader opens for anyone who can't edit, and for editors with **View mode** on.
  - Name controls exactly: **Fit all**, **Center axis**, **Search** (`/`), **Separate by tags**, **Rejoin**, **Full screen** (`F`), **Time display**.

## ACT 1 · THE MARKS (0:00 – 0:52)

### Scene 1 · 0:00 – 0:16 · A line of time

**FRAME:** A horizontal axis with a ruler: tick labels "1490 · 1500 · 1510". Title above: "A voyage". On the right, a small legend card that will fill in as the marks are named.

**MOTION:** The axis draws; ticks fade in along it.

**OST:** "Time runs left to right."

**VO:**
"A timeline is a line of time. Earlier is on the left, later on the right, and the ruler along the line tells you where you are, whether that's years, days or seconds."

### Scene 2 · 0:16 – 0:52 · Dots, bands, numbers and arcs

**FRAME:** The sample fills in: (1) a dot with a stem and a title, "Leaves port"; (2) a band beneath the axis, "The crossing", spanning from that dot to a later one; (3) a dot with a number "4" in it where four moments crowd together; (4) an arc above the axis joining two dots. Each joins the legend card as it's named: "Moment", "Duration", "Summary", "Relationship".

**MOTION:** Each mark draws on its word and pulses once; its legend row ticks in at the same time.

**OST:** "Moments, durations, summaries, relationships."

**VO:**
"A dot is a moment: one point in time, with its title. A band is a duration: a stretch with a start and an end. A dot with a number is a summary: moments too close together to draw apart at this zoom, and the number says how many. And an arc above the line is a relationship, linking two things that are connected."

## ACT 2 · MOVING AROUND (0:52 – 1:38)

### Scene 3 · 0:52 – 1:14 · Zoom and pan

**FRAME:** The sample timeline. The gesture inset shows a scroll wheel and two fingers pinching apart. The summary "4" splits into four titled dots as the view zooms in; the ruler changes from years to months.

**MOTION:** Zoom centred on the summary; the number dissolves into four dots that spread apart. Then a drag (cursor, one finger) pans right.

**OST:** "Scroll or pinch to zoom. Drag to move."

**VO:**
"To zoom, scroll, or pinch with two fingers side by side. Zoom in on a summary and it opens up into the moments inside it. Drag to move along the line, or up and down. On a keyboard, the arrow keys pan and plus and minus zoom."

### Scene 4 · 1:14 – 1:38 · Getting your bearings

**FRAME:** The toolbar drawn as a strip of buttons: "Search", "Separate by tags", "Time display", "−", "＋", "Fit all", "Center axis", "Full screen". Each lights as it's named. A small "/" key cap beside Search and an "F" key cap beside Full screen.

**MOTION:** Each button lights as it's named; **Fit all** zooms the sample back out to the whole voyage.

**OST:** "Fit all. Search. Full screen."

**VO:**
"Lost? Fit all shows the whole timeline again. Search, or the slash key, finds moments by their words and takes you there. Full screen, or F, gives the timeline the whole window, and Escape brings the page back."

## ACT 3 · OPENING WHAT'S THERE (1:38 – 2:30)

### Scene 5 · 1:38 – 2:02 · The reader

**FRAME:** A finger taps the dot "Landfall". A reader panel opens over the right half: a picture at the top with a small credit line, the time, tag chips "voyage", "landfall", the notes as formatted text, and a "Related to" list with one entry, "Leaves port". On a phone silhouette inset, the same reader fills the screen.

**MOTION:** The panel slides in from the right; choosing the related entry swaps the panel's content to that moment.

**OST:** "Click anything to read it."

**VO:**
"Click or tap a moment or a band to read it: its pictures, its time, its tags, its notes, and what it's related to. Choose a related item to read that one next. On a phone, the reader fills the screen."

### Scene 6 · 2:02 – 2:18 · Tracks and displays

**FRAME:** Left: **Separate by tags** pulls the moments tagged "storm" into a second track above the first; a **Rejoin** button below. Right: the same ruler read three ways, stacked: "1492-10-12", "1492.78 years", "-15059606400" (the stored seconds since 1970).

**MOTION:** The tagged dots lift into their own track; then the three rulers flip in one after another.

**OST:** "Pull out a thread. Read time your way."

**VO:**
"Separate by tags pulls one thread, like every storm, into a track of its own; Rejoin puts it back. And a timeline's time display decides how its times read: as dates, as years, or as exact numbers."

### Scene 7 · 2:18 – 2:30 · Over to you

**FRAME:** The sample timeline, fully explored. The end card: "Open a public timeline · no account needed", the ◷ openchronology wordmark, and "Next: tours of real timelines". The end card also gives the source: "github.com/openchronology/timeline-app".

**MOTION:** The sample zooms out to Fit all; the end card rises and holds.

**OST:** "Now open one."

**VO:**
"That's all it takes to read any timeline. Open a public one, no account needed, and look around."

## Appendix C · Questions this audience is likely to ask

1. **Why does a number show instead of my moment?** It's a summary; zoom in or click it to list what's inside.
2. **Why can't I change anything?** You're reading a timeline you can't edit, or **View mode** is on.
3. **Can I make the dots bigger?** Ctrl-scroll, a trackpad pinch, or a pinch with one finger above the other scales the contents.
